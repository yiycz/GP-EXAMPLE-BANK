## US–Soviet Nuclear Deterrence During the Cold War

Throughout the **Cold War (approximately 1947–1991)**, the United States and Soviet Union accumulated vast nuclear arsenals and developed **second-strike capabilities**, creating a situation of **Mutually Assured Destruction (MAD)** in which a nuclear first strike could provoke devastating retaliation against the aggressor. Despite intense geopolitical rivalry and the **1962 Cuban Missile Crisis**, the two superpowers avoided direct large-scale warfare, with nuclear deterrence widely regarded as an important factor in discouraging military escalation by making the potential costs of conflict catastrophically high.

**Use:** The credible threat of overwhelming military retaliation can deter aggression and preserve strategic stability, demonstrating that the balance of power and mutual vulnerability may prevent direct conflict even between deeply antagonistic states.
