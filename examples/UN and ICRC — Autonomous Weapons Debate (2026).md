## UN and ICRC — Autonomous Weapons Debate (2026)

In August 2026, the UN Secretary-General and ICRC President renewed calls for legally binding international rules on autonomous weapons, warning that technological development was advancing rapidly and that a race toward greater autonomy in weapons was already under way. States have debated the issue within the UN framework for years, but progress has been slow because governments remain divided over whether new binding rules are necessary.

**Use:** This illustrates why IOs can remain relevant despite great-power rivalry: competition creates incentives for states to pursue military advantage independently, while a universal forum such as the UN provides a venue for negotiating common rules that could constrain destabilising arms competition and preserve wider international stability. Without the UN, major powers could simply pursue their own military interests independently. Through the UN framework, however, states are brought together to debate shared limits, clarify how international law should apply, and potentially create binding rules that constrain everyone.
