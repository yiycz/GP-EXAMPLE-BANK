## Our SG Heritage Plan 2.0 

Our SG Heritage Plan 2.0 is Singapore's second five-year master plan for the heritage and museum sector, building on the original plan from 2018 to 2022. It seeks to preserve both tangible heritage, such as historic buildings and artefacts, and intangible heritage, such as cultural traditions, practices and crafts.

**Identity — UNESCO recognition of the kebaya**
e.g. Singapore collaborated with Brunei, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand to nominate the kebaya for UNESCO recognition. On 4 December 2024, it was successfully inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity.

Effectiveness: The successful inscription demonstrates tangible international recognition of Singapore's multicultural heritage, helping raise awareness of traditional craftsmanship while reinforcing Singapore's shared cultural identity with its Southeast Asian neighbours.


**Community — Heritage Activation Node**
In April 2024, NHB launched its first Heritage Activation Node in Katong–Joo Chiat, offering community-organised heritage tours, cultural activities and exhibitions, before expanding to other neighbourhoods

By early 2026, the three nodes at Katong–Joo Chiat, Clementi and Punggol had attracted approximately 30,000 visitors and involved over 360 volunteers, around 60% of whom were youths. This demonstrates measurable participation in local heritage activities and the involvement of younger generations in heritage preservation.


**Industry — SG Heritage Business Scheme**
Launched in March 2025, the SG Heritage Business Scheme recognises established traditional businesses and provides branding, marketing and tailored business support to help preserve their commercial and cultural relevance.

By early 2026, 42 heritage businesses had received official designation. These include traditional businesses such as Tong Heng Delicacies. The scheme helps increase their visibility and could attract new customers, thereby improving their prospects of long-term survival.




**Innovation — Singapore Odyssea**
In August 2025, the National Museum of Singapore opened Singapore Odyssea: A Journey Through Time, an immersive exhibition combining multimedia projections, interactive storytelling and RFID wristbands to take visitors through 700 years of Singapore's history.

The operational exhibition demonstrates how technology can transform historical artefacts and narratives into interactive experiences, potentially making Singapore's history more appealing and understandable to younger, digitally accustomed audiences. It is consistent with the plan's Innovation pillar, although evidence of improved historical understanding has not been established
