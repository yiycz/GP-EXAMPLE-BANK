## WHO Emergency Declaration — COVID-19 (2020)

When COVID-19 emerged in late 2019, the virus spread internationally within weeks. Yet under the WHO’s International Health Regulations, declaring a Public Health Emergency of International Concern (PHEIC) required **formal assessment and consultation**. The WHO Emergency Committee first met on 22–23 January 2020 but was **divided**; the emergency was declared only on 30 January. An independent review later concluded that the **formal notification** and **emergency-declaration procedures** were too slow for such a **fast-moving respiratory pathogen**, and that valuable time was lost.

**Use:** Both examples show that international organisations may be slow for different structural reasons – bureaucracy in one case and **political deadlock** in the other – but the result is similar, that global crises today can escalate faster than international organisations can produce decisive collective action, thereby contributing to the view of international organisations not being relevant today.
