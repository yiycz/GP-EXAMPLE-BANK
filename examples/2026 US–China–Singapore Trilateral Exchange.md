## 2026 US–China–Singapore Trilateral Exchange

Defence Minister Chan Chun Sing explicitly described Singapore in September 2026 as a third party perceived as honest, trusted and serious, rather than simply a provider of meeting facilities.

As political scientist Joseph Nye noted, soft power relies on a nation's culture, political values, and legitimate foreign policies. For other countries to find those elements attractive, they must first believe that the nation is authentic and credible.

In August 2026, Singapore's RSIS convened more than 40 representatives and experts from the US, China and Singapore to discuss managing geopolitical tensions, with participants from both powers acknowledging Singapore's contribution as a trusted platform for candid dialogue.

**Use:** This demonstrates how Singapore's international credibility translates into diplomatic soft power, enabling a small state to facilitate exchanges between rival superpowers and contribute to international discussions without relying on coercive power. Singapore's reputation as a credible and impartial diplomatic partner constitutes a significant source of soft power, enabling it to facilitate dialogue between rival powers.
