## Stanford Prison Experiment

In **1971**, psychologist Philip Zimbardo recruited 24 male college students and randomly assigned them to act as either “prisoners” or “guards” in a simulated prison at Stanford University. The behaviour of some guards rapidly became abusive and participants experienced significant psychological distress, causing the planned two-week experiment to be terminated after only six days.

**Use:** This supports the argument that scientific experiments can produce unpredictable and harmful consequences, demonstrating why researchers must prioritise participants’ welfare and maintain strong ethical safeguards even when pursuing potentially valuable knowledge.
