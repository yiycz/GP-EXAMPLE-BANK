## Indonesia's Forest Fires and Transboundary Haze (2015)

In **2015**, widespread forest and peatland fires in Indonesia, partly caused by **deliberate slash-and-burn agricultural practices used to clear land for palm oil plantations**, destroyed approximately **2.6 million hectares of land** and generated severe transboundary haze affecting neighbouring countries, including Singapore and Malaysia. According to the **World Bank**, the fires inflicted approximately **US$16.1 billion in economic damages and losses, equivalent to 1.9% of Indonesia's GDP**, disrupting **agriculture, tourism, transportation and manufacturing**, demonstrating how preventable environmental degradation can impose economic costs far exceeding the short-term benefits of environmentally destructive practices.

**Use:** The pursuit of short-term economic gains at the expense of environmental protection can undermine long-term economic growth, as preventable environmental damage destroys productive resources and disrupts economic activities.
