## UN OCHA — Myanmar Earthquake Relief (2025)

After the 7.7-magnitude earthquake struck Myanmar on 28 March 2025, relief had to be delivered across **a country already fragmented by civil war, damaged infrastructure and restricted humanitarian access**. The United Nations, through **OCHA**, coordinated the overall response through established national and sub-national humanitarian clusters, bringing together UN agencies, NGOs and local organisations across sectors such as health, food, shelter and water. By mid-2025, Myanmar’s wider humanitarian response involved 332 partner organisations, including 115 national NGOs, 108 civil-society organisations, 78 international NGOs and 13 UN agencies.

This coordination produced assistance on a scale no single actor was positioned to deliver across all sectors: the earthquake response ultimately reached 1.7 million people, while different partners provided medical care, clean water, food and shelter across affected regions.

Use: This demonstrates the relevance of international organisation in not just delivering aid itself, but also in providing the **coordination architecture** that **allowed hundreds of specialised actors to operate as a collective system**. Without such an international coordinating mechanism, relief efforts would be more **fragmented**, duplicative and likely to leave important needs or locations uncovered.
