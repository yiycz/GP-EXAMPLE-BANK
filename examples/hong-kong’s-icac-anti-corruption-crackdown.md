## Hong Kong’s ICAC Anti-Corruption Crackdown

Corruption was deeply entrenched in Hong Kong's public sector during the 1960s and early 1970s, including within the police force. In 1974, the government established the **Independent Commission Against Corruption (ICAC)**, which combined aggressive law enforcement with corruption prevention and public education. Its sustained efforts helped transform Hong Kong from a corruption-ridden society into one widely regarded as having a clean public administration.

**Use:** This supports the argument that entrenched corruption can be substantially reduced when governments establish independent institutions with strong enforcement powers and pursue sustained institutional reform.
