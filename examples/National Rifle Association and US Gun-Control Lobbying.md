## National Rifle Association and US Gun-Control Lobbying

The National Rifle Association (NRA) and the wider gun lobby have spent millions of dollars in donations and campaign support for American politicians, particularly Republican lawmakers, in exchange for their opposition to gun control. As a result, even after repeated mass shootings in recent years — including school shootings in Nashville (2023) and Georgia (2024) — Congress has failed to pass meaningful gun reform, despite polls consistently showing that a majority of Americans support stricter measures such as universal background checks. Ordinary citizens thus watch their elected representatives side with a powerful, well-funded lobby over the clear wishes of the majority. This visible gap between what voters want and what politicians deliver breeds deep cynicism, as many Americans conclude that their leaders are loyal to their donors rather than genuinely committed to protecting the public.

**Use:** Many politicians are closely linked to wealthy donors and powerful interest groups, whose influence can outweigh ordinary citizens’ interests. This fuels public cynicism, as voters may see politicians as self-serving rather than genuinely committed to the public good.
