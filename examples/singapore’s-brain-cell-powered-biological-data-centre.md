## Singapore’s Brain-Cell-Powered Biological Data Centre

In **2026, NUS Medicine, DayOne and Cortical Labs launched Singapore’s first Biological Data Centre prototype** at the NUS Life Sciences Institute. Instead of relying solely on conventional silicon processors, the system uses living human neurons grown from stem cells and connected to silicon hardware to process information. Its 20-unit CL1 biological computing system explores whether such “wetware” can perform computing and AI-related tasks using substantially less energy than conventional digital infrastructure.

**Use:** This supports the argument that scientific innovation can radically redefine the boundaries of technology by combining biological and artificial systems, potentially making AI more energy-efficient while also raising new ethical questions about the use of living human cells in computing.
