## Singapore leader

Singapore's Lee Kuan Yew is widely credited with building one of the cleanest civil services in the world, partly by paying public servants very high salaries so they would not be tempted by graft. The same leader, in the same era, is also widely criticised for cracking down hard on press freedom and political opposition. Both are well documented. Neither cancels the other out. On the other hand, strongman leaders such as Suharto of Indonesia, where corruption was endemic, surrounded themselves with other ministers loyal only to him, who then abused their positions because their positions were secure.

**Use:** The rise and presence of villains or heroes in politics today often depends on the cultures and prevalent political systems within a given society. Strong, principled governments with third party oversight will weed out self-serving politicians and encourage the proliferation of ethical leaders, while weak governments will allow for the rise in corrupt politicians through cronyism and nepotism.
