## Ethnic Integration Policy (EIP)

Singapore introduced the **Ethnic Integration Policy (EIP) in 1989**, imposing ethnic quotas at the HDB block and neighbourhood levels to prevent racial enclaves from developing in public housing. When the quota for a particular ethnic group has been reached, some resale transactions involving buyers from that group may be restricted. As around 80% of Singapore’s population lives in public housing, the policy deliberately creates opportunities for different racial groups to live and interact alongside one another.

**Use:** This supports the argument that governments may justifiably restrict some individual choices to promote racial integration and social cohesion, although such intervention can also impose costs on homeowners whose resale options are limited. This also shows Singapore govnerment is paternalistic because the state **limits individual housing choices on the assumption that enforced racial mixing produces a better social outcome**.
